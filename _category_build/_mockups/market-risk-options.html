Market & Risk — layout options

Options for every panel on Market & Kraljic, Porter Five Forces and Risk Register, plus the case for collapsing the three into one. Illustrative content; the point is the shape, not the numbers.

The condensation question

Those three screens answer one question: what is the supply market doing to us, and what does that mean for how we buy? Kraljic gives the structural position, Porter explains why that position exists, and the risk register says what would change it. Split across three tabs, the reader holds the Kraljic verdict in their head while reading Porter, then holds both while reading risk.

Merging alone would put 10 panels on one screen, which is worse. Two are duplicates: Pricing Environment is the unsourced version of Market Intelligence, and Net Buyer Leverage is nothing more than the read of the Five Forces. Fold those and it is 7 panels in three named bands, which is exactly what the section-divider rule is for.

Option 3 — keep three subtabs, restyle only10 panels · no merge

The conservative path: leave the structure alone and fix only the panel layouts below. Costs nothing to reverse, but keeps the duplication (Pricing Environment vs Market Intelligence, Net Buyer Leverage vs Five Forces) and keeps the argument split across three clicks.

Kraljic — B: position on a plotted axisshows how far into the quadrant

A grid says which box. A plot says where in the box, and how close to tipping into the next one. Useful when the argument is "we are leverage today, but only just".

Category positionplotted
Close to the vertical divide: supply risk is rising with megavendor bundling. Another cycle of consolidation moves this category toward Strategic, where the play changes.
Porter — A: scored rowsCurrent, merged with the verdict

Bar length is the score, so the strongest constraint is obvious without reading. Sorting strongest-first means the top row is always the thing to negotiate against.

Porter — B: radarshape at a glance, harder to read a value

A radar shows the shape of the market in one mark, which is good for comparing two categories side by side. It is worse at answering "how strong is supplier power exactly", and five axes on a small chart get cramped. I would use this only if category-to-category comparison becomes a job this dashboard has to do.

Ranks by likelihood × impact and puts mitigation, trigger and owner inside each row. One panel instead of three, and nothing is hidden — but you lose the at-a-glance read of which risks cluster in the act-now corner.
