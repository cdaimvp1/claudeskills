Kraljic & Porter — segmentation

You are right that one dot and one radar for "Software" say nothing. Both frameworks only earn their keep when the things inside a category land in different places and get different plays. The question is what to segment by, and the answer depends on what the data can actually carry.

What the data supports today, and what it does not

Two of the three segmentations you described already exist in the seed, hiding in the subcategory names. The third is the one worth asking IT Finance for, and it is the one that matters most for AI cost control.

On goods versus services. Software is both, and the line-item dimension splits accordingly. The goods-like part prices as unit × rate (seats, GB, tokens, API calls) and belongs on a consumption tag. The service-like part prices as role × rate × hours (implementation, managed service, support) and belongs on a rate card. A single "line item" toggle has to carry both, which is why the field to ask for is a unit type plus a unit count, not a free-text description.

Each dot is a segment, sized by spend. The toggle changes what a dot means without changing the frame, so the quadrants keep their meaning and only the grain changes. The category average stays on as a hollow ring so you can see how far each segment sits from it.

Why this is worth the change. The category average sits in Leverage, so the one-dot version said "compete it". Segmented, two of the six are in Strategic: Medicines Development and Scientific Research have few credible alternatives and high switching cost, and competing them is the wrong play. That distinction is invisible at category level and it is the single most consequential thing on this screen.
Porter, per segment, sharing the same toggle

You are right that one radar is boring — because it is an average, and the average of a monopoly and a commodity market is a market that does not exist. Per segment, the shape changes hard, and the shape IS the finding. Same toggle as Kraljic, so the two panels always agree on what a segment is.

Supply-market forcesPorter · by business purpose
Five axes, clockwise from top: rivalry, supplier power, substitutes, new entrants, buyer power. Larger area toward an axis means that force is stronger.

What the shapes say

  • Medicines Development is supplier-dominated and thin on substitutes. Competing it is theatre. Leverage comes from the migration milestones, not a threat.
  • Marketing / Sales is balanced and contested. This is where a real competition changes the price.
  • Collaboration has high rivalry and strong substitutes, and you already pay for one of them inside the Microsoft EA. This is a retire-and-consolidate market, not a negotiation.
Selecting a segment filters the Kraljic dot, the force profile and the risk register together, so the whole band answers for one segment at a time.

What it means for this category

  • Model renewals at 15%, not at inflation. A budget built on 5% will be wrong on the largest agreements in the portfolio.
  • Price the AI clause set before the commercial terms. They gate the deal and take longer to land than the discount does.
  • Ask what the AI-free SKU costs, in writing, even with no intention of buying it. A documented refusal is leverage at the next renewal.
  • Consolidate deliberately. Removing the last credible alternative converts leverage into dependency, and the price follows a cycle later.

What I would ask IT Finance for
One field, two columns A unit type and a unit count on each invoice or rate-card line: seats, GB, tokens, API calls, instance-hours, support hours, role-days. That one addition turns on the line-item toggle for both panels, makes AI spend separable from licence spend for the first time, and is the only view in which "AI is driving up cost" stops being a market claim and becomes a Lilly number.
