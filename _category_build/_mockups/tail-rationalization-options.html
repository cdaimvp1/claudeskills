You are right that these overlap. Overlap & Consolidation (Rationalization) asks "are we buying the same capability more than once". Tail Consolidation Opportunities (Tail & Contracts) asks "are we buying small things from too many people". Both end in the same move, fewer suppliers, and both currently list opportunities in the same shape. Merged, the screen answers one question: where are we buying the same thing more than once, and what is it worth to stop.

Option B — effort-versus-value scatter, list below the sequencing question, answered visually

The list tells you what things are worth. It does not tell you what to do first. Plotting value against effort does: the top-left quadrant is where you start, and it is visible before anyone reads a row. Bubble size is the number of suppliers removed, so the biggest circles are the ones that also simplify the estate.

Where to start value against effort · bubble = suppliers removed
Two of the three largest bubbles sit bottom-right: the biggest supplier-count reductions are also the hardest and the least valuable. That is the finding, and a ranked list hides it. The money is in capability overlap; the effort is in tail sprawl.
Option C — capability map, duplication shown not asserted strongest for "why are we buying this twice"

Rows are capabilities, cells are suppliers. Where a row has more than one filled cell you are paying more than once, and you can see it without reading a number. Best at making the case to a business owner who does not accept that their tool is a duplicate; weakest at sequencing.

The decision: where does Contract Opportunities go? It is renewal windows and off-contract spend, which is contract exposure and not consolidation.
