EU/UK Data Residency CommitmentPlaybook S14.2 - Data Privacy & Cross-Border Transfer
Red LineNo Data
Lilly Position: All Lilly Information and Usage Data originating from EU/UK data subjects must be processed and stored exclusively within EU/UK data centers; no cross-border transfer to Nimbus's US-based processing environment without a valid transfer mechanism (Standard Contractual Clauses) reviewed by Lilly Legal.
Why Non-Negotiable: GDPR Articles 44-49 cross-border transfer restrictions; Lilly's Global Privacy Standard requires in-region processing for EU/UK personal data absent an approved transfer mechanism.
Relevant Regulation: GDPR (EU) 2016/679, UK GDPR.
Supplier History: No prior history; this is a new commitment not previously requested of Nimbus. Category benchmark is approximately 55% first-pass acceptance for cloud infrastructure suppliers when data residency is requested at renewal (Medium confidence, category-level pattern).
If Supplier Pushes Back:
  1. Cite GDPR Articles 44-49 and Lilly's Global Privacy Standard; in-region processing is the default posture, not a negotiable enhancement.
  2. Escalate to Cyber_ISS_Review@Lilly.com for a technical feasibility discussion if Nimbus claims their EU region lacks capacity or feature parity.
  3. Final position: this is a Hard Stop; walk away if necessary.
SME Contact: Cyber_ISS_Review@Lilly.com - Pre-Engagement: Recommended (non-standard scope; engage before first draft exchange).
Sanctions & Export Control CompliancePlaybook S02.1 - Regulatory & Trade Compliance
Red LineAccepted
Lilly Position: Nimbus and any subcontractor or fourth-party processor must represent and warrant it is not a Sanctioned Person, is not owned or controlled by a Sanctioned Person, and will not use any Sanctioned Person or Sanctioned Country infrastructure to deliver the Services.

Relevant Regulation: OFAC, FCPA.
Supplier History: Nimbus accepted equivalent language in the 2024 MSA amendment without material redline; standard boilerplate for this supplier.

72-Hour Breach Notification TriggerPlaybook S14.7 - Data Security & Incident Response
Red LineRejected
Lilly Position: Nimbus must notify Lilly within 72 hours of discovering any actual or reasonably suspected security incident affecting Lilly Information, regardless of Nimbus's own internal materiality assessment.

  2. Escalate to the Global Procurement Attorney if Nimbus argues the WO scope falls outside the MSA's incident definition.
  3. Final position: this is a Hard Stop; walk away if necessary, though precedent strongly favors holding without escalation.

Tier 2 - Open with standard. Defend vigorously. Fallback only under significant pressure and approval.
Liability Cap Tied to Fees Paid
Hold Firm
Lilly Standard Position: Aggregate liability capped at 2x total fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim, with an uncapped carve-out for confidentiality breach, IP infringement, and gross negligence / willful misconduct.
Acceptable Fallback: 1.5x fees paid, provided the carve-outs remain fully uncapped.
Unacceptable: A flat cap below 1x annual fees, or any cap that folds the confidentiality / IP / gross-negligence carve-outs inside its ceiling.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 62% (N=8, Medium confidence) - category-level pattern for managed hosting suppliers of similar size.
Supplier-Specific History: Nimbus opened at 1x fees paid with no carve-outs in the 2022 MSA negotiation; settled at 2x with carve-outs after two rounds.
Predicted Pushback: "Our standard terms cap liability at 1x annual fees across all engagements, including this renewal."
Compliance Leverage: Finding CD-2026-0417, $140K in rate card violations (Section 05). Talking point: "Given the rate-card discrepancies identified in the current term, a liability structure with real carve-outs protects both parties going forward."
Negotiation Guidance: Open at 2x with full carve-outs. If Pushed: cite the 2022 precedent. Fallback Trigger: after two rounds of resistance. Trade Value: High - do not trade without a Tier 2 or better return.
Termination for Cause Cure Period
Hold Firm
Lilly Standard Position: 15-day cure period for material breach, with immediate (no-cure) termination rights for a breach of confidentiality, data protection, or sanctions / export-control obligations.
Acceptable Fallback: 30-day cure period for general material breach; no-cure carve-outs remain non-negotiable.
Unacceptable: A cure period longer than 30 days, or extending cure rights to the no-cure carve-out categories.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 71% (N=7, Medium confidence).
Supplier-Specific History: No Nimbus-specific data; category benchmark applied.
Predicted Pushback: "45 days is standard in our master services agreements with enterprise clients."
Negotiation Guidance: Open at 15 days. If Pushed: move to 30 days. Fallback Trigger: if Nimbus ties this to a Tier 3 concession. Trade Value: Medium - good candidate to receive a Tier 3 trade.
Data Deletion & Return on Termination
Hold Firm
Lilly Standard Position: Nimbus must return or certify destruction of all Lilly Information within 30 days of termination, with a signed certificate of destruction and no retention exception beyond standard backup rotation (max 90 days, disaster-recovery purposes only).
Acceptable Fallback: 45 days for return / destruction if Nimbus documents a specific technical constraint.
Unacceptable: An indefinite or undefined backup retention window, or return contingent on final invoice payment.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 58% (N=6, Medium confidence).
Predicted Pushback: "Our backup systems retain data for up to 12 months as part of standard disaster recovery architecture."
Negotiation Guidance: Open at 30 days. If Pushed: 45 days with a documented constraint. Fallback Trigger: only with written technical justification. Trade Value: Medium.
Service Level Credits & Remedies
Hold Firm
Lilly Standard Position: Monthly SLA credits scaled by severity (up to 15% of monthly fees for sustained Severity 1 outages), with a right to terminate for cause after 3 consecutive months of missed Severity 1 SLAs.
Acceptable Fallback: 10% cap on monthly credits if the 3-strike termination trigger is preserved.
Unacceptable: SLA credits as sole and exclusive remedy with no termination trigger regardless of repeated failures.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 66% (N=5, Medium confidence).
Supplier-Specific History: Nimbus accepted a 12% credit cap with a 3-strike trigger in the 2024 amendment for a different work order.
Predicted Pushback: "Credits are the sole remedy under our standard SLA framework; we don't offer termination rights tied to service levels."
Negotiation Guidance: Open at 15%. If Pushed: cite the 2024 amendment precedent (12%). Fallback Trigger: settle at 10-12% only if the 3-strike trigger holds. Trade Value: Medium-High.
Subcontractor Flow-Down Obligations
Hold Firm
Lilly Standard Position: All Lilly-specific obligations (data protection, sanctions compliance, confidentiality, audit rights, breach notification) must flow down in writing to every subcontractor and fourth-party processor Nimbus uses, with Nimbus remaining fully liable for subcontractor performance.
Acceptable Fallback: Flow-down limited to subcontractors with direct access to Lilly Information, provided Nimbus retains full liability.
Unacceptable: Any flow-down exception leaving a data-access subcontractor outside the obligation set, or any dilution of Nimbus's liability for subcontractor acts.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 69% (N=6, Medium confidence).
Predicted Pushback: "Some of our infrastructure partners operate under their own compliance frameworks and won't sign bespoke flow-down language."
Negotiation Guidance: Open at full flow-down. If Pushed: narrow to data-access subcontractors. Fallback Trigger: only with full retained liability. Trade Value: Medium.

Strategic Trade

Insurance Aggregate Limits
Strategic Trade
Standard Position: $10M aggregate cyber liability coverage, $5M per-occurrence.
Fallback Position: $7M aggregate if per-occurrence stays at $5M.
Risk of Fallback: Low-Medium - reduced aggregate ceiling on a low-frequency risk given MSA Section 9 minimums already verified adequate at the per-occurrence level.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 52% (N=4, Medium confidence).
Predicted Outcome: Likely settles at $7M-$8M aggregate.
Trade Strategy: Best Traded For: Liability Cap Tied to Fees Paid (Tier 2). Framing: "We have room to move on the aggregate insurance limit if the liability cap structure with full carve-outs stays intact." Timing: Round 2.
Force Majeure Definition Scope
Strategic Trade
Standard Position: Force majeure limited to events genuinely beyond either party's reasonable control; explicitly excludes Nimbus-side labor disputes and third-party supplier failures within Nimbus's control to remediate.
Fallback Position: Include Nimbus-side labor disputes if Nimbus commits to a business continuity / failover plan disclosure.
Risk of Fallback: Low - primarily a definitional scope concession with a compensating disclosure obligation.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 44% (N=5, Medium confidence).
Predicted Outcome: Likely settles with labor disputes included in exchange for continuity plan disclosure.
Trade Strategy: Best Traded For: Termination for Convenience retention (part of the Tier 2 termination position set). Framing: "We can broaden the force majeure definition if Lilly retains unrestricted termination for convenience." Timing: Round 2 or Final Round.
Publicity & Reference Rights
Strategic Trade

Fallback Position: Standing approval for a single pre-agreed reference-customer listing (name only, no logo, no case study detail), revocable at any time.
Risk of Fallback: Low - limited brand exposure, easily revocable.
Historical Acceptance Rate: 51% (N=4, Medium confidence).
Predicted Outcome: Likely settles at the name-only reference-listing fallback.
Trade Strategy: Best Traded For: Data Deletion & Return on Termination (Tier 2). Framing: "We can offer a limited reference listing if Nimbus tightens the post-termination data return timeline." Timing: Round 1 or Round 2.

Negotiation Tactics View (Legal Terms)

Cross-cut of the same tier-classified positions above, re-read by legal term instead of by tier. Introduces no new positions or data.

Liability
Your Position: Aggregate cap at 2x fees paid (12 months), uncapped carve-outs for confidentiality, IP, and gross negligence (Liability Cap Tied to Fees Paid, Tier 2).
Argument Options: (1) The cap scales proportionally with the relationship's size and risk. (2) The uncapped carve-outs protect only the highest-severity failure modes. (3) The $140K rate-card finding shows why a liability structure with real teeth matters.
Likely Supplier Pushback: "Our standard terms cap liability at 1x annual fees across all engagements."
Your Rebuttal: The 2022 MSA precedent already established 2x with carve-outs for this relationship; this WO should not regress below that precedent.
Fallback: 1.5x fees paid if carve-outs remain fully uncapped.
Indemnity
Your Position: Subcontractor flow-down preserves Nimbus's full liability for subcontractor acts (Subcontractor Flow-Down Obligations, Tier 2).
Argument Options: (1) Lilly's relationship is with Nimbus, not its subcontractors; liability cannot be diluted by a supply-chain layer Lilly did not select. (2) Flow-down scoped to data-access subcontractors is a reasonable, auditable boundary.
Likely Supplier Pushback: "Some infrastructure partners won't sign bespoke flow-down language and operate under their own frameworks."
Your Rebuttal: Nimbus's liability for those partners is unaffected by what the partners will sign directly with Lilly; that is a subcontractor-management problem for Nimbus, not a basis for weakening Lilly's protection.
Fallback: Flow-down limited to data-access subcontractors, with full retained liability.
IP
Your Position: Standard Lilly IP ownership terms apply to Work Product; no new position generated at the WO level (playbook resolved at the MSA level).
Argument Options: (1) MSA-level IP terms already cover WO deliverables under the existing Work Product definition. (2) No WO-specific customization of Nimbus's platform creates joint IP.
Likely Supplier Pushback: None anticipated - no historical friction on this term with Nimbus.
Your Rebuttal: Not applicable; hold as-is per the MSA.
Fallback: Not applicable.
Data / Privacy
Your Position: EU/UK Data Residency Commitment and the 72-Hour Breach Notification Trigger (both Red Line, Section 01).
Argument Options: (1) GDPR Articles 44-49 and Article 33 are baseline regulatory floors, not optional enhancements. (2) The MSA's confidentiality clause (Section 14) covers data handling in principle but not physical residency, which is why this surfaces at the WO level.
Likely Supplier Pushback: "Our EU region may not have full feature parity" (residency); "internal investigation timelines need 5 business days" (breach notification, per 2022 history).
Your Rebuttal: Feature-parity gaps are a scoping/technical discussion, not a basis to relax the residency requirement; the 2022 MSA already set the 72-hour precedent.
Fallback: None - both are Hard Stops.
Termination (including Termination for Convenience)
Your Position: 15-day cure period with no-cure carve-outs (Termination for Cause, Tier 2); Force Majeure scope excludes Nimbus-side labor disputes unless a continuity plan is disclosed (Tier 3).
Argument Options: (1) A short cure period protects Lilly's operational continuity for R&D-supporting infrastructure. (2) Termination-for-convenience retention is reasonable given the multi-year renewal term.
Likely Supplier Pushback: "45 days is standard in our enterprise MSAs."
Your Rebuttal: 30 days is already a fallback concession from Lilly's 15-day opening; 45 days exceeds Lilly's operational risk tolerance for R&D-supporting infrastructure.
Fallback: 30-day cure period; broadened force majeure scope in exchange for continuity plan disclosure and retained termination-for-convenience rights.
Warranties
Your Position: Data Deletion & Return on Termination functions as a post-term warranty obligation; Service Level Credits & Remedies functions as an ongoing performance warranty (both Tier 2).
Argument Options: (1) A defined destruction certificate closes the loop on data warranties at exit. (2) SLA credits paired with a termination trigger create a real performance warranty, not just a rebate mechanism.
Likely Supplier Pushback: "Backup systems retain data up to 12 months" (deletion); "credits are the sole remedy" (SLA).
Your Rebuttal: A documented technical constraint can support a modest extension (30 to 45 days) but not an indefinite window; sole-remedy credits without a termination trigger leave Lilly with no recourse for chronic failure.
Fallback: 45-day deletion window with documented justification; 10-12% credit cap with the 3-strike termination trigger preserved.

Finding CD-2026-0417$140,000 impact - Severity: Medium-High

  1. Raise the $140K finding factually at the start of Round 2, before any liability cap discussion.
  2. Tie it explicitly to the existing MSA Section 11 audit rights - this strengthens an already-resolved provision rather than opening a new one.
  3. Use it as the stated rationale for holding firm on the Liability Cap Tied to Fees Paid position specifically, not as general pressure across unrelated terms.
Framing Note: Deploy factually, not punitively - frame as "ensuring the new agreement prevents the issues we have both experienced," not as an accusation.
